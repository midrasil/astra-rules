# Complications

**Complications** are narrative problems tied to the character. They create people, situations and consequences that can interfere with their story, without applying penalties or mechanical modifiers.

A Complication can represent an enemy, a pursuit, an obligation, a secret, a troublesome reputation, a difficult relationship or another lasting situation able to cause the character trouble.

## Narrative nature

Complications create situations in the story, instead of making the character numerically worse. They cannot:

- apply dice penalties;
- reduce Attributes, Skills or derived traits;
- cause automatic failures;
- mechanically limit actions or equipment;
- directly change combat, damage, Willpower or other resources;
- grant rewards when they come into play.

A Complication's only mechanical touchpoint is the cost to resist a [behavioral Complication](#behavioral-complications).

The lack of a reward is intentional. It is up to the Narrator to make Complications show up in the story. When a Complication is relevant, the Narrator can introduce characters, events and consequences tied to it. This should come from the fiction and does not need to happen every session.

## Behavioral Complications

Some Complications, such as **Big Mouth**, **Arrogant** or **Phobia**, describe impulses, beliefs or patterns of behavior. They guide how the character is played and can influence their actions.

When a situation directly pressures that behavior and the player wants to act against it, the character can resist by taking **1 point of superficial [Willpower](../Personagens/Vontade.md) damage**. There is no roll. The cost is the same for Minor and Major Complications, and only goes up when the Narrator decides the situation is extreme.

By paying the cost, the character resists the Complication at that moment. With a full Willpower track, they can still resist by [Breaking Limit](../Personagens/Vontade.md#break-limit), as long as there is superficial damage on the track.

If the character does not pay the cost, the Complication shows itself in a way that fits the situation. The Narrator sets the Complication's immediate consequence, but does not take general control of the character beyond the behavior being tested.

For example, if a character with **Big Mouth** is captured and pressured to reveal information, they can take 1 point of superficial Willpower damage to stay quiet. If they do not pay, they let some relevant information slip.

## Types

A Complication can be **Minor** or **Major**. The difference is set by its reach and narrative importance.

### Minor

A Minor Complication has limited reach, shows up occasionally and usually involves one person, a small group or a specific place. Its consequences are manageable and usually complicate a scene, relationship or secondary goal.

### Major

A Major Complication has broad reach or a recurring presence. It can involve an organization, region or powerful threat, put allies and important goals at risk and sustain a campaign arc.

## Available types

Each entry in the [List of Complications](Lista%20de%20Complica%C3%A7%C3%B5es.md) says which types are available:

- **Minor:** exists only as a Minor Complication;
- **Major:** exists only as a Major Complication;
- **Minor or Major:** the player chooses one of the two types when acquiring the Complication.

## Character creation

During [character creation](../../Cria%C3%A7%C3%A3o%20de%20Personagem/4%20-%20Privil%C3%A9gios%20e%20Complica%C3%A7%C3%B5es.md), each character must acquire exactly **4 Complication Points**.

| Type acquired | Value |
|---|---:|
| **Minor** | 1 Complication Point |
| **Major** | 2 Complication Points |

## Customization

Each Complication presents a general concept that should be customized for the character. When acquiring it, the player and the Narrator set together the elements needed for it to exist concretely in the campaign.

Depending on the Complication, this can include:

- its origin and reason to exist;
- the people, organizations or communities involved;
- what those parties want from the character;
- its reach and the places where it matters;
- relationships or events tied to it;
- connections with other elements of the sheet and the story;
- examples of how it can show up during play.

These elements should fit the type acquired and be recorded with the Complication. They can be expanded or changed as new information comes up during the campaign.

## Acquiring during the campaign

When the story's events produce a new lasting problematic situation, the Narrator can give the character a Complication.

## Transformation and resolution

Complications are not removed by spending XP, points or other abstract resources. They can be reduced, transformed or removed when the situation they represent changes in the story.

If a Complication available as Minor or Major changes severity, the new type replaces the old one. Resolving a Complication can also create another: defeating an Enemy, for example, can result in a pursuit or in hostility from their former allies.

The **Resolution** section of each entry presents narrative possibilities, not a complete list of requirements. Following one of these possibilities does not automatically remove the Complication. Its final resolution is decided during play by the player and the Narrator, according to events and their consequences.

## Format of a Complication

Each Complication uses the following format:

- **Name**
- **Type** — Minor, Major or Minor or Major.
- **Description** — presents the Complication, its narrative consequences, the difference between the available types and the elements the player and the Narrator should set to customize it.
- **Resolution** — presents possibilities for how the Complication can be reduced, transformed or resolved, without setting an automatic solution.
