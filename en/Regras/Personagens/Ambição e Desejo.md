# Ambition and Desire

Characters are defined not only by their abilities, but also by what they want to achieve.

**Ambition** and **Desire** represent the goals that guide a character's decisions during the story. Both show what they are after, but they work on different scales.

**Ambition** represents a long-term goal, while **Desire** represents something the character means to achieve more immediately.

Each character has **1 Ambition** and **1 Desire** at a time.

These elements help set clear motivations for the character and give the Narrator tools to create conflicts, opportunities and consequences tied to the players' interests.

## Ambition

**Ambition** represents an important, lasting goal for the character.

Usually, an Ambition cannot be completed in a single scene or session. It can take several steps, decisions or events before it is reached.

An Ambition can be about power, knowledge, revenge, recognition, protection, wealth, discovery or any other goal that matters to the character.

Examples of Ambitions include:

- winning a position of influence;
- finding a missing person;
- destroying a rival organization;
- uncovering the origin of a mysterious phenomenon;
- recovering a lost object;
- protecting a community;
- becoming recognized as the best in a given field.

An Ambition should be broad enough to stay relevant for a significant part of the story, but specific enough that you can tell when the character is moving toward it.

### Progressing toward an Ambition

An Ambition does not need to be completed all at once.

The character can take actions that represent **significant progress** toward their goal.

Uncovering an important piece of information, removing an obstacle, winning an ally or completing a necessary step can all represent progress toward an Ambition.

At the end of a session in which the character **actively** worked toward their Ambition, **1 point of aggravated Willpower damage** becomes **superficial damage**. This can happen **once per session**.

When an Ambition is finally completed, the player should choose a new Ambition suited to the character and to the current moment of the story.

## Desire

**Desire** represents what the character means to achieve right now.

Unlike Ambition, a Desire should be relatively immediate and can usually be completed during a session or over a few scenes.

The Desire is set **at the start of each session**. The player can keep the previous session's Desire or choose a new one, which does not need to be related to the previous one or to the character's Ambition, although it can be.

A Desire can come from the story's current circumstances, from a relationship with another character or from an opportunity that comes up during play.

Examples of Desires include:

- convincing someone to reveal a piece of information;
- getting access to a restricted area;
- impressing an important person;
- recovering an item;
- finding out who is following the group;
- winning a negotiation;
- humiliating a rival;
- finding a way into a building.

A good Desire should represent something the character really wants to achieve and that can influence their decisions during the session.

### Fulfilling a Desire

Once per session, when the character acts decisively to **fulfill** their Desire, they recover **1 point of superficial [Willpower](Vontade.md) damage** — immediately, at the moment of the action.

Fulfilling the Desire **ends** it.

## Changing Ambitions and Desires

Ambitions and Desires are not permanent.

Important events can change a character's priorities.

A character can abandon an Ambition after finding out it is no longer possible, realizing their goal has lost its importance or going through an experience that transforms their motivations.

The Desire can only be changed **at the start of a session**.

The Ambition should change less often, since it represents a deeper and more lasting motivation of the character.

## Ambition and Desire in the Story

Ambitions and Desires do not work only as individual goals.

They also help build the campaign's narrative.

The Narrator can use the characters' Ambitions to create opportunities, opponents, allies and conflicts tied to the group's goals.

Desires, in turn, help steer the characters' more immediate decisions during each session.

When different characters have incompatible Ambitions or Desires, that can also create interesting conflicts within the story itself.

The purpose of these traits is not to dictate how a character must act, but to show what they consider important at that moment.

> **Ambition represents what the character means to achieve over the course of the story.**
> 
> **Desire represents what they want to achieve now.**
