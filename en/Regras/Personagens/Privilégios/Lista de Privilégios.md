# List of Privileges

This is the list of core Privileges. They all follow the [Privilege rules](Privil%C3%A9gios.md).

In this list, a **social test** is any test whose Pool uses [Charisma](../Atributos.md#charisma).

## Ally

**Description:** the character has a close relationship with an NPC willing to help them. At Level 1, the Ally is a competent person with limited reach. At Levels 2 and 3, they have increasingly relevant experience, connections or resources. At Levels 4 and 5, they are a powerful, exceptional figure or one able to influence far-reaching events. The player and the Narrator should set who the Ally is, how the relationship began, why they help the character, what resources they have and what their limits are.

### Specializations

#### Specialist

The player chooses a field of the Ally's expertise. When they use [Help](../../Mec%C3%A2nica%20B%C3%A1sica/Reserva%20de%20Dados.md#help) in that field, they add **2 dice** to the Pool instead of 1.

#### Available

The Ally can be found and mobilized easily. When circumstances allow communication, the character can contact them without needing to look for them or find out where they are.

## Appearance

**Description:** the character's presentation shapes the first impression they make. Appearance can involve physical beauty, clothing, presence, posture, etiquette or other visible elements. At Levels 1 and 2, the character has a polished or notable presentation. At Level 3, their presence stands out and is easily remembered. At Levels 4 and 5, their appearance is exceptional or iconic and can command the attention of a room. The player and the Narrator should set which traits make up this Appearance and how they are usually perceived.

### Specializations

#### Attractive

The character adds **2 dice** to social tests in which their appearance works in their favor.

#### Famous Face

The character looks like someone famous and adds **2 dice** to social tests in which that resemblance works in their favor.

#### Innocent

The character looks innocent and blameless. At the Narrator's discretion, they add **2 dice** to tests related to avoiding suspicion or deflecting blame.

#### Striking Feature

The character has a rare, memorable feature, such as an unusual eye color or a singular build. They add **2 dice** to social tests with strangers, but suffer a **1 die** penalty when trying to disguise themselves.

## Contacts

**Description:** the character knows people who can provide information, make introductions or ease access to certain circles. At Level 1, they have contacts in a small, specific circle. At Levels 2 and 3, their network reaches a city, different trades or a region. At Levels 4 and 5, it includes influential people, spans great distances and reaches circles that are usually inaccessible. The player and the Narrator should set in which environments the Contacts operate, what information they usually have and how these relationships are kept up.

### Specializations

#### Informants

The character adds **2 dice** to tests made to get information through their network of Contacts.

#### Black Market

The Contacts make it possible to find restricted, illegal or hard-to-find goods and services. The Specialization does not provide these resources for free: getting them still depends on the character's Resources and the circumstances of the story.

## Fame

**Description:** the character is known and admired by a certain audience. At Level 1, they are recognized within a small community or specific circle. At Level 2, their name is known in a city or relevant circle. At Level 3, their Fame reaches a region or nation. At Level 4, it crosses borders. At Level 5, the character is a world-famous or legendary figure. The player and the Narrator should set the origin of the Fame, why the character is admired, who usually recognizes them and where their name matters.

## Languages

**Description:** every character knows the common language of their region by default. Each Level in this Privilege grants knowledge of **one additional language**. The player must choose and record each language known. `Languages 1`, for example, grants one language besides the local common one, while `Languages 5` grants five.

## Influence

**Description:** the character can mobilize people and institutions to act in their favor. At Level 1, they hold influence over a community or local trade. At Level 2, they can affect the decisions of a city or relevant institution. At Level 3, they reach a region. At Level 4, they interfere in the decisions of a nation or large organization. At Level 5, their influence crosses countries and can change the fate of peoples. The player and the Narrator should set the origin of this Influence, over whom it works, what means the character uses and what costs or consequences its use can produce.

### Specializations

#### Intermediaries

The character can exert their Influence through third parties. Their involvement does not become evident just because the Influence was used, although investigations or consequences can reveal it.

#### Institutional Pressure

The character adds **2 dice** to social tests in which they concretely use their Influence over an institution able to affect the situation.

## Mentor

**Description:** the character has a relationship with a more experienced NPC who offers guidance, knowledge and advice. At Level 1, the Mentor is experienced in a limited field. At Levels 2 and 3, they are a recognized authority with important knowledge and connections. At Levels 4 and 5, they are among the greatest masters of their field or have historical importance. The player and the Narrator should set who the Mentor is, what their field of knowledge is, how the relationship began, why they guide the character and what limits they have.

### Specializations

#### Counselor

When the character can consult the Mentor before a test related to the Mentor's field of knowledge, they add **2 dice** to the Pool.

#### Instructor

When the Mentor uses [Help](../../Mec%C3%A2nica%20B%C3%A1sica/Reserva%20de%20Dados.md#help) within their field of knowledge, they add **2 dice** to the Pool instead of 1.

## Prestige

**Description:** the character holds a recognized position within a specific organization. At Level 1, they are a known and trusted member. At Level 2, their opinion is heard and they can ask ordinary members for help. At Level 3, they hold a position of authority. At Level 4, they are among the main leaders. At Level 5, their name and decisions represent the organization itself. The player and the Narrator should set what the organization is, how the character earned their position, what responsibilities they have and how the members treat them.

### Specializations

#### Restricted Access

The character can enter the organization's restricted places, meetings or archives when their position justifies that access.

#### Requisition

The character can temporarily request equipment, personnel or other resources belonging to the organization. The Narrator decides what it has, the time needed and the responsibilities the requisition creates.

## Resources

**Description:** the character has access to assets, income and goods that represent their wealth. Resources do not correspond directly to available money and can be spread across investments, property, businesses, rights or inheritances. At Level 1, the character keeps a comfortable life and has some financial security. At Level 2, they are well-off. At Level 3, they control significant assets and investments. At Level 4, they have a great fortune. At Level 5, they control extraordinary or dynastic wealth. The player and the Narrator should set the origin of the wealth, how it is distributed and how it can be accessed.

### Specializations

#### Liquidity

A relevant part of the wealth can quickly be turned into available money, without depending on lengthy negotiations or selling important assets.

#### Credit

The character can acquire goods and hire services based on their assets and financial reputation, even when they have no money available at that moment.

#### Hidden Fortune

Part of the wealth is hard to locate, link to the character or confiscate. The character adds **2 dice** to tests made to hide their connection to these assets.

## Haven

**Description:** the character has a place they can use as a residence, protection and base of operations. At Level 1, it is a small room, shelter or hideout. At Level 2, it can be a house or establishment with space and privacy. At Level 3, it is a large property, with grounds, staff or several buildings. At Level 4, it can house and protect a large group, like a fortress. At Level 5, it represents a domain, large complex or exceptional territory. The player and the Narrator should set the Haven's location, appearance, origin, occupants and concrete capabilities.

### Specializations

#### Guards

People protect the Haven and react against intruders. The number, training and equipment of the Guards should match the Haven's Level.

#### Security

The Haven has locks, alarms, traps, false passages or other planned protections. When an occupant uses this structure to detect or stop an intrusion, they add **2 dice** to the relevant test.

#### Armory

The Haven stores weapons, armor, ammunition and other equipment suited to the setting. The quantity and overall quality of the stock follow the Haven's Level.

#### Library

The Haven has a collection of books, records or other sources of knowledge. The player and the Narrator set the subjects covered by the collection according to the Haven's Level. Research, Scholarship or Occultism tests made on site with the Library's help get **2 dice**.

#### Laboratory

The Haven has space, instruments and materials for experiments, analyses or specialized work. Research, Scholarship or Occultism tests that use the Laboratory get **2 dice**.

#### Infirmary

The Haven has space, supplies and facilities to care for the wounded. Healing tests made in the Infirmary get **2 dice**.

#### Mystic Ward

The Haven has a supernatural protection defined by the player and the Narrator. The strength of the protection and the creatures, forces or phenomena it can hold back should match the Haven's Level.

#### Sanctuary

The Haven has a place dedicated to a faith, cult or tradition, suited for prayers, meditations, ceremonies and rituals.

#### Cell

The Haven has a locked, reinforced space to hold prisoners. Its capacity and security follow the Haven's Level.

#### Escape Route

The Haven has a hidden exit or an alternative path, such as a tunnel, secret passage or access to the sewers.

#### Prime Location

The Haven is located in an exclusive, strategic or especially convenient place, set when the Specialization is acquired.

#### Luxury

The Haven offers comfort, decoration and conditions suited for receiving important people. Its standard follows the Haven's Level.

## Followers

**Description:** the character has loyal people who accompany them, serve them or act on their behalf. At Level 1, they have an assistant or companion. At Level 2, they count on a few servants or helpers. At Level 3, they keep an entourage with different abilities. At Level 4, they have a large retinue able to manage tasks and properties. At Level 5, they keep a court or personal organization that spreads their will to distant places. The player and the Narrator should set who the Followers are, why they are loyal, what tasks they perform and what limits they have.

### Specializations

#### Experts

The Followers are trained in a field set when the Specialization is acquired. They can perform ordinary tasks in that field and, when they use [Help](../../Mec%C3%A2nica%20B%C3%A1sica/Reserva%20de%20Dados.md#help) in it, they add **2 dice** to the Pool instead of 1.

#### Armed

The Followers have weapons, armor or other equipment suited to the setting and can protect the character, their property or their interests.

#### Spread Network

The Followers are spread across different places within the Privilege's reach and can observe events or carry out tasks far from the character.

#### Discreet

The Followers know how to carry out tasks without drawing unnecessary attention. When they make a test to act unnoticed or keep their actions from being linked to the character, they get **2 dice**.

## Status

**Description:** the character holds a recognized position in society, such as a title, social class or rank. At Level 1, their position has local importance or matters within a limited circle. At Level 2, they belong to a municipal or regional elite. At Level 3, they hold a high position within a region or nation. At Level 4, they are among the most important figures in society. At Level 5, their position is sovereign, dynastic or recognized across different peoples. The player and the Narrator should set the origin of the Status, where it is recognized, what duties come with it and how other people treat the character.

### Specializations

#### Legal Authority

The character's position grants them recognized authority within a jurisdiction, allowing them to issue orders and make decisions consistent with their Status.

#### Legal Immunity

Their position offers protection against certain forms of detention, trial or interference. This protection has limits set by the setting and can have political consequences when used.

#### Preferential Treatment

The character receives priority, hospitality and access in environments that recognize their Status.
