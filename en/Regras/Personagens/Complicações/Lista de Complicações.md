# List of Complications

This is the list of core Complications. They all follow the [Complication](Complica%C3%A7%C3%B5es.md) rules and produce only narrative consequences.

## Obsessive Admirers

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character attracts one or more people whose admiration goes beyond reasonable limits. They can follow the character, show up at inconvenient moments, invade their privacy, spread stories or put themselves in danger to get their attention. The player and the Narrator should set who these admirers are, why they became interested in the character, what they want and what limits they are willing to cross.

**Resolution:** setting boundaries, driving the admirers away, helping them let go of the obsession or changing the circumstances that feed their fixation are some possibilities. Reacting aggressively or humiliatingly can end the admiration, but can also turn it into resentment or enmity. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Cursed

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character carries a supernatural curse that interferes with their life. As a Minor Complication, its manifestations are occasional, have limited reach or affect specific situations. As a Major Complication, the curse is persistent, dangerous and central to the character's story. It creates narrative events and consequences, without applying numerical penalties. The player and the Narrator should set the curse's origin, how it manifests, who else is involved and what seems necessary to break it.

**Resolution:** fulfilling a condition, making amends for the act that caused the curse, confronting whoever cast it, performing a ritual or accepting a transformation are some possibilities. Transferring the curse can solve the character's problem, but also create new consequences. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Illiterate

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character cannot read or write in any language, although they may recognize common symbols, simple signs or their own signature. Texts, records, written maps and messages may require other people's help or a workaround. This creates narrative situations, but applies no penalties to tests. The languages the character knows, including those from the Languages Privilege, are spoken only. The player and the Narrator should set how illiteracy is part of the character's story, who knows about it, who usually helps them and in which contexts it can become relevant.

**Resolution:** learning to read and write with the help of a teacher, institution or companion is the most direct possibility. Finding a permanent way to access written information can also transform the Complication, depending on the story. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Disliked

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** a group of people dislikes the character because of some trait, origin or identity. This rejection can come from racism, homophobia or another prejudice against something the group sees in them. As a Minor Complication, it is a limited group whose dislike causes hostility and difficulties within a specific circle. As a Major Complication, the rejection comes from a broad or influential group able to harm important relationships and opportunities. The player and the Narrator should set what the group is, what it rejects in the character, how it shows that dislike and how far its influence reaches.

**Resolution:** making amends for an offense, clearing up a misunderstanding, earning respect or forging an alliance with someone important in the group are some possibilities. Leaving that community or accepting a final separation can also end its relevance. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Arrogant

**Type:** Major

**Description:** the character is convinced of their own superiority and feels the need to show it whenever they meet someone able to rival them. Merely winning may not be enough: they want to make it clear they could dominate or humiliate their opponent. The player and the Narrator should set in which field the character considers themselves superior, where that certainty comes from, how they treat possible rivals and which relationships are affected by their arrogance.

**Resolution:** suffering a defeat they cannot explain away, honestly acknowledging a rival's skill, depending on someone they looked down on or understanding the harm caused by their attitude are some possibilities. Learning humility can reduce their arrogance or turn it into another trait. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Haunted

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** a spirit or other supernatural presence follows the character and interferes with their life. As a Minor Complication, its manifestations are occasional and cause limited problems. As a Major Complication, the presence is active, persistent and able to threaten important relationships or goals. Unlike Sinister Haven, the haunting is tied to the character and follows them. The player and the Narrator should set who or what the presence is, why it is tied to the character, what it wants, how it manifests and who can perceive it.

**Resolution:** discovering and fulfilling the presence's wish, reconciling with it, revealing its story, banishing it or breaking the bond that ties it to the character are some possibilities. Destroying a manifestation may not resolve the cause of the haunting. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Big Mouth

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character has trouble keeping secrets and often reveals information that should stay private, especially when excited, irritated or trying to impress someone. When pressured to stay quiet, they can resist by taking 1 point of superficial Willpower damage, as with every [behavioral Complication](Complica%C3%A7%C3%B5es.md#behavioral-complications). If they do not pay, they let some relevant information slip. The player and the Narrator should set what kind of information they usually reveal, who knows about this behavior and which relationships or responsibilities can be harmed by it.

**Resolution:** learning to act with discretion, making a serious commitment to silence or changing after facing the consequences of a revelation are some possibilities. Trust earned or lost during the campaign can also transform the Complication. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Cautious

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character is overly careful, avoids hasty decisions and tries to plan every detail before acting. Their hesitation can delay a response, create conflicts with allies or let an opportunity slip away. The player and the Narrator should set where this caution comes from, in which situations it shows most strongly and how their companions deal with this behavior.

**Resolution:** learning to trust their own instincts, coming to trust their companions or living through a situation in which delay has important consequences are some possibilities. Overcoming the cause of their caution can also change the way they act. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Conflict with a Faction

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character has a direct feud with a faction, organization or institution. They may have stolen its resources, killed one of its members, interfered with its interests or provoked the conflict some other way. As a Minor Complication, the faction has limited reach or devotes few resources to the dispute. As a Major Complication, it is powerful and actively bent on harming the character. The player and the Narrator should set what the faction is, what caused the feud, what it wants from the character and what resources it is willing to mobilize.

**Resolution:** proving loyalty, making amends for an offense, working together against a common threat, negotiating with the leadership or bringing about an internal change in the faction are some possibilities. Destroying or leaving the organization can also end the conflict, but its consequences can create new enemies. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Curious

**Type:** Major

**Description:** the character feels a constant need to investigate mysteries, unknown places and situations it would be safer to leave alone. An unanswered question can lead them into dangerous affairs or into meddling with other people's secrets. The player and the Narrator should set what kinds of mystery spark their curiosity, why they need to find answers, what limits they still respect and who usually gets dragged into their investigations.

**Resolution:** discovering the origin of this need, suffering consequences that change their relationship with the unknown, finding answers to a central question or learning to accept that some mysteries should remain unsolved are some possibilities. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Delusional

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character believes in something other people consider absurd or impossible. As a Minor Complication, they usually keep this belief to themselves or let it interfere only in specific situations. As a Major Complication, they express and follow this belief openly, letting it influence important decisions and their relationships. The player and the Narrator should set what the belief is, how it came about, what events seem to confirm it and who knows about this conviction.

**Resolution:** finding evidence able to change their belief, understanding the experience that gave rise to it or discovering a truth different from what they imagined are some possibilities. If the belief turns out to be true, the Complication can be resolved or transformed according to the consequences of that discovery. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Death Wish

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character wishes to die after completing an important goal. They do not try to throw their life away in just any situation, but accept great risks when they believe they face a chance to fulfill that goal or find a death they consider meaningful. The player and the Narrator should set what the goal is, why the character wishes to die after reaching it, what risks they are willing to accept and which people know about their intention.

**Resolution:** completing or abandoning the goal, finding a new reason to live, coming to terms with the event that gave rise to this wish or realizing their death would harm someone important are some possibilities. Fulfilling the goal does not automatically mean the character's death or the resolution of the Complication. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Overconfidence

**Type:** Major

**Description:** the character believes they can overcome any challenge and often takes greater risks than common sense would advise. They do not necessarily wish to die, but they underestimate dangers, ignore warnings and are slow to recognize when they need to back off or ask for help. The player and the Narrator should set where this confidence comes from, which abilities it rests on, what kinds of challenge usually make them overreach and which people try to rein them in.

**Resolution:** suffering a significant defeat, recognizing their own limits, learning to trust their companions' judgment or taking responsibility for the consequences of a reckless decision are some possibilities. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Phobia

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character has an irrational fear tied to something specific. As a Minor Complication, the fear causes hesitation, discomfort or attempts to avoid situations related to it. As a Major Complication, the fear is central to their decisions and can lead them to abandon plans, flee or depend on other people when faced with it. Phobia applies no penalties to tests. The player and the Narrator should set the object of the fear, its possible origin, how the character usually reacts and who knows about this vulnerability.

**Resolution:** facing the fear in meaningful circumstances, understanding or overcoming its origin, receiving support from someone important or undoing a supernatural cause are some possibilities. A single encounter with the source of the Phobia does not automatically mean overcoming it. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Greedy

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character places great importance on wealth and measures part of their worth by what they own or win. As a Minor Complication, they usually argue over rewards, demand their share and favor lucrative opportunities. As a Major Complication, they can put relationships, important goals or their own safety at risk to get what they consider rightfully theirs. The player and the Narrator should set what form of wealth they want, why it is so important, what they consider a fair split and what limits they are still unwilling to cross.

**Resolution:** finding another measure of their own worth, choosing a relationship over a great reward, losing something because of their greed or achieving a wealth that does not satisfy their desires are some possibilities. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Enemy

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** a specific NPC bears enmity toward the character and wants to harm them. As a Minor Complication, the Enemy has reach and influence similar to the character's. As a Major Complication, they have great reach, influence or resources and can threaten the character's allies and important goals. The player and the Narrator should set who the Enemy is, the origin of the enmity, what they want and what resources, relationships and limits they have.

**Resolution:** defeating, reconciling or negotiating with the Enemy, removing the cause of the conflict or taking away their means and interest to continue are some possibilities. Their death can end the Complication, but allies, heirs or the consequences of their actions can turn it into another one. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Bad Reputation

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** damaging stories about the character influence how other people treat them. As a Minor Complication, they are badly spoken of within a small circle, where they meet distrust, contempt or closed doors. As a Major Complication, their infamy reaches a region or broad social circle, drawing hostility, threats and unwanted attention. The player and the Narrator should set what event gave rise to this reputation, who knows the story, what people believe about the character and where it has influence.

**Resolution:** clarifying the events, repairing the harm caused, earning the trust of the people affected or doing something able to change public opinion are some possibilities. The reputation may also lose importance when the character leaves that circle. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Quirk

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character has a recurring habit, preference or behavior that seems small but occasionally causes problems. The Quirk should be specific and cannot replace another Complication that better represents the same behavior. The player and the Narrator should set what the Quirk is, when it usually shows up, how other people react to it and what kind of situation it can complicate.

**Resolution:** dropping the habit, understanding and overcoming its origin or replacing it with a behavior that no longer causes problems are some possibilities. A Quirk can also simply stop being relevant due to the changes the character goes through. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Possessed

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** a supernatural entity inhabits or is bound to the character and tries to influence them or act through them. As a Minor Complication, its influence comes up occasionally through voices, urges or brief manifestations. As a Major Complication, the entity interferes repeatedly, tries to take control at important moments and threatens the character's relationships and goals. The Complication does not give the Narrator unrestricted control over the character. The player and the Narrator should set what the entity is, how the possession happened, what it wants, how its presence manifests and what the agreed limits on its actions are.

**Resolution:** casting out or imprisoning the entity, negotiating with it, fulfilling what keeps it bound to the character or undoing the event that allowed the possession are some possibilities. Separating the entity may reveal that part of its influence remained or create another Complication. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Wanted

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character is wanted by an authority, organization or faction. As a Minor Complication, the pursuit is limited to a city, small group or distant region and can lead to questioning, arrests or difficulties when they are recognized. As a Major Complication, the character is actively hunted in the campaign's region, and their pursuers have the resources to track their movements, threaten allies and disrupt their plans. The player and the Narrator should set who is after them, the reason, the reach of the pursuit and what their pursuers intend to do if they find them.

**Resolution:** proving their innocence, serving a sentence, receiving a pardon, negotiating with the pursuers or revealing the true culprit are some possibilities. Fleeing, changing identity or eliminating whoever leads the pursuit can change the situation, but can also turn it into another Complication. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Compromised Haven

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the security or secrecy of the character's haven has been compromised. This Complication does not require the Haven Privilege: without it, it refers to the place where the character lives or shelters. Someone knows its location, has a way in or knows something that can be used against its occupants. The player and the Narrator should set who compromised the haven, how it happened, what information or access that person has and what they intend to do with it.

**Resolution:** moving to another haven, changing its security, recovering stolen access, negotiating with whoever knows the place or convincing them to keep quiet are some possibilities. Eliminating a known threat may not solve the problem if the information has already been shared. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Sinister Haven

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** something about the character's haven causes fear, unease or distrust. This Complication does not require the Haven Privilege: without it, it refers to the place where the character lives or shelters. As a Minor Complication, its appearance, history or atmosphere drives visitors away and makes meetings there difficult. As a Major Complication, a supernatural presence, curse or manifestation inhabits the haven and can act at important moments. The player and the Narrator should set the origin of the sinister aspect, how it manifests, who knows its history and what any presence tied to the place wants.

**Resolution:** restoring the place, understanding its history, undoing a curse, appeasing or banishing a presence and abandoning the haven are some possibilities. Resolving one manifestation may reveal another cause behind it. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Secret

**Type:** Minor or Major

**Description:** the character hides information that could change how other people treat them. As a Minor Complication, its revelation can cause shame, loss of reputation or the estrangement of an ally. As a Major Complication, the secret involves something serious, such as a crime, a betrayal, a forbidden identity or an event able to destroy their position and threaten people close to them. The player and the Narrator should set the secret, who could discover it, who would be affected and what clues could lead to the truth.

**Resolution:** voluntarily revealing the truth, repairing its consequences, earning the forgiveness of those involved or making the information irrelevant are some possibilities. Silencing witnesses or destroying evidence can protect the secret, but does not necessarily resolve the Complication. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.

## Stubborn

**Type:** Minor

**Description:** the character insists on doing things their own way and has great difficulty admitting they are wrong. This attitude can drag out arguments, prevent agreements or lead them to stand by a decision even in the face of its consequences. The player and the Narrator should set on which subjects their stubbornness shows most strongly, what usually makes them insist and which relationships are affected by this behavior.

**Resolution:** acknowledging an important mistake, learning to trust someone else's judgment or facing the consequences of their insistence are some possibilities. A change of values or perspective can also help them let go of this behavior. The final resolution depends on the fiction and should be decided in play by the player and the Narrator.
